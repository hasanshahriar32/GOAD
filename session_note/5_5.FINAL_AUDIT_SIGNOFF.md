## 2. Mathematical Audit & Resolution of the Stackelberg Inversion

### 2.1 Theoretical Diagnosis: Why the Phase 5 Inversion Occurred
In game-theoretic security literature (Guo et al., AAAI 2022/2023; APR 2025):
- **A Static/Myopic Greedy Baseline** evaluates edge path centrality $\phi_{G_0}(e) / c(e)$ **once** on the unhardened initial directory graph $G_0$, greedily revoking top edges within budget without anticipating adversarial rerouting. Because enterprise identity graphs feature multiple group memberships and parallel delegation chains, static edge cuts leave backup paths open while draining budget on high-cost upstream `MemberOf` edges ($c(e) \approx 3.0$).
- **The Co-Adaptive Stackelberg Policy** solves the bi-level leader-follower interdiction problem by dynamically tracking the follower's best-response reachability subgraph $G_{\text{BR}} \subseteq G_{\text{curr}}$ and prioritizing sink-adjacent bottleneck chokepoints (`Group \to Template` `Enroll`/`WriteDacl`, `Template \to CA`, and `Template \to Tier-0` escalation links). Severing a single downstream edge of low disruption cost ($c(e) \approx 1.0\text{--}1.3$) simultaneously severs all upstream low-privilege attacker trajectories.
