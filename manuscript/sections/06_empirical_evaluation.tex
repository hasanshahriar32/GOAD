\section{Empirical Evaluation and Live Cyber-Range Benchmarks}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We conduct an empirical evaluation to validate CertGraph's static gating, dynamic provenance tracking, and game-theoretic interdiction. Specifically, we address four core research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Inductive Shortcut Cure):} Does Multiplicative Bilinear Gating eliminate shortcut memorization and prevent accuracy collapse on adversarial hard negatives?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Provenance Validity):} Which rollback and split-actor certificate lineages are supported by captured Windows evidence, and how do incomplete identity fields, optional-source faults, and time uncertainty affect attribution?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Provenance Scalability):} What processing cost and compression are observed on the configured stress stream, and which retention and reasoning costs remain unresolved?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Operational Interdiction Efficiency):} Does the Co-Adaptive Stackelberg policy achieve superior path interdiction while minimizing administrative disruption compared to industrial greedy heuristics?
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Synthetic Identity Graphs:} The historical static benchmark uses 700 class-balanced synthetic identity graphs. Additional graph sizes are used in the separate scalability experiments. Neither the class proportions nor the graph generator constitute a measured enterprise distribution. The new matched gating audit uses the existing two-step implementations with fixed source snapshots and separately recorded training, validation, and test inputs.

\paragraph{Live Cyber-Range Cyberbed:} We use a virtualized Game of Active Directory (GOAD) cyber-range. Fresh inventory of the restored DC and CA identifies Windows Server 2016 Standard Evaluation, build 14393. That measured inventory supersedes the earlier draft's Server 2019 description. The two nodes are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{DC03} (\texttt{essos.local}): Active Directory Domain Controller, Kerberos KDC, and LDAP directory.
    \item \texttt{SRV03} (\texttt{braavos.essos.local}): Certificate Authority running Microsoft Active Directory Certificate Services.
\end{itemize}
The new split-actor case uses domain accounts \texttt{ESSOS\textbackslash khal.drogo} and \texttt{ESSOS\textbackslash viserys.targaryen}. Original disk images were read-only; execution used writable overlays. The capture pipeline runs non-interactively, while restoring the old VM images required console-assisted recovery. This measured 2023 patch level is not described as a current patched configuration.

\subsection{RQ1a: In-Distribution Ablations, Calibration, and Perturbation Asymmetry}
We first distinguish the historical class-balanced generator from the newer attribution and live-policy experiments. Their labels, model implementations, and observation units differ.

\paragraph{Empirical Confirmation of Theorem~\ref{thm:theorem1} (Skip-Connection Ablation):}
The saved five-fold artifact reports Macro-F1 $0.9986 \pm 0.0029$ with skips and $0.4768 \pm 0.0239$ without skips. Recomputing the paired statistic from the five saved fold values gives $t=46.245$, $p=1.308\times10^{-6}$, and four degrees of freedom. The dispersions are population standard deviations across these folds. Shared training data and generator assumptions limit interpretation of this descriptive comparison. An empirical ablation does not validate the theorem outside its coefficient-independence and derivative assumptions.

\paragraph{Class Proportions and Probability Interpretation:}
The static experiments reported here are generator-based classification studies. Their balanced labels do not establish a 96\% enterprise safe base rate, and their softmax scores are not calibrated probabilities of real-world compromise. We retain probabilities and selected checkpoints in the matched audit so that classification errors can be checked independently.

\paragraph{Perturbation Asymmetry and Simple Comparators:}
The saved robustness artifact gives Macro-F1 $0.9479$ after 30\% edge perturbation, compared with $0.8965$ and $0.7709$ after 5\% and 10\% feature perturbation. This sensitivity pattern motivates testing structural counterfactuals; it does not prove that every prediction ignores topology. A separately verified, previously saved three-seed ESC13 abstraction also favors simple graph-count models over the existing GNN on its IID cases. Its symbolic reference defines the labels, so its perfect agreement is a consistency result rather than independent exploit validation.

\subsection{RQ1b: Static GNN Generalization and Adversarial Hard Negatives}
A source audit identifies two problems with interpreting the historical $41/63$ safe-label agreement as a general hard-negative detection result. Ten ``deep horizon'' cases retain a membership path to an allowed enrollment group and therefore are positive under a transitive effective-permission interpretation. The stored 22 disagreements are eight administrator-only write cases, four deny-write cases, seven benign OID links, and three deep OID links; the earlier OID/CA explanation did not match that artifact. The canonical generator also repeats inputs across training and validation. These historical counts are retained in the artifact but do not establish independent-case specificity or a binomial confidence interval.

We therefore freeze and execute a matched $2\times2$ diagnostic: additive versus feature-channel-gated implementations, each with counterfactual augmentation probability zero or 0.3, across three new seeds. Model equations, propagation depth, and original artifacts remain unchanged. Each seed evaluates 250 opposite-label families, with membership depths 0, 1, 2, 4, and 8, randomized node order, and matched configuration features. Positive members retain reachable enrollment; negative members either restrict enrollment to the administrator or add a reachable explicit deny. Queue traversal and Boolean closure independently agree on all test labels under this stated ESC1 permission abstraction. CA trust and resolved permissions are assumed; Windows exploitability is not the label definition.

\input{generated/gating_evidence}

The gate gives zero false positives but misses all 450 positive cases at depths 2, 4, and 8 across the three seeds. Adding the same augmentation to the additive implementation improves its pooled accuracy from 50\% to 71.67\%, compared with the gate's 70\%. Thus the audit does not establish superiority of gating over an augmentation-matched comparator. First-step membership transforms are fixed in these existing NumPy implementations, and their feature-channel gate is $\sigma(W_{\mathrm{gate}}\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}+b)$, rather than a literal implementation of the general bilinear edge scorer in Section~\ref{sec:certgraph_arch}. Initialization also differs between implementations. These distinctions prevent attributing the entire contrast to a bilinear operation.

Only 22 unique training inputs occur per seed, with 21 also present in validation. Consequently, validation selection in this diagnostic is not an independent-distribution generalization test. All 6,000 saved test predictions are reproduced from saved checkpoints, and their labels and aggregates are checked independently. A symbolic second tier must also examine unresolved or out-of-horizon cases: verifying only neural-positive candidates cannot recover these observed neural false negatives.

\paragraph{Literal bilinear realization and family-disjoint validation:}
We address the implementation mismatch with a separate literal realization of the bilinear edge and self-skip equations: two relational-attention layers, four heads, 32 hidden channels, type-specific input projection, and fully trainable encoder, attention, skip, and gate parameters. The binary readout evaluates the stated ESC1 resolved-permission predicate, not observed Windows compromise. Additive and gated arms start with identical parameter values and receive matched augmentation. Neither symbolic closure nor outcome labels enter the forward pass. Gradient, node-relabeling, and padding checks validate implementation properties; the historical implementations remain unchanged.

The new generator constructs varied reachable and unreachable membership branches and paired allow/deny interventions. For each of three new seeds, 300 training and 100 validation families are separated from 150 families in each of IID, long-path, and larger-background test cohorts. Typed five-round Weisfeiler--Lehman input hashes and family IDs are disjoint across splits within a seed. This conservatively excludes isomorphic whole inputs under the encoded roles and edge types, without claiming disjoint local motifs. Long-path positive enrollment chains have membership depth 2, 4, or 8. The larger-background cohort adds isolated nodes to different generated families; it is not a matched causal estimate of graph-size effects or enterprise-scale generalization.

\input{generated/bilinear_evidence}

Independent NetworkX reachability checks all 5,100 saved case labels, and checkpoint inference reproduces all 10,800 held-out predictions. The literal bilinear and additive models have identical classifications in every matched arm: IID accuracy averages $86.00\%\pm2.19\%$, larger-background accuracy $87.00\%\pm1.00\%$, and long-path accuracy 50\%. In each arm, all 450 positive long-path cases are missed. Some IID negatives also require deny-membership reasoning beyond the local horizon; 126 pooled false positives remain. Probability scores differ, but their reported calibration errors concern these synthetic cohorts only. This closes the literal-implementation and repeated-whole-input gaps while failing to establish classification superiority for bilinear gating. Exact verification of unresolved cases remains necessary under the stated permission contract.

\paragraph{Enterprise Concurrency Mechanics and Sensitivity Trade-Offs:}
As documented in Table~\ref{tab:cipg_stress} and Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity_matrix}, in the configured synthetic CA/KDC schema model ($\pi_{\text{CA}}(e_{4887}) \cap \pi_{\text{KDC}}(e_{4768}) = \emptyset$), operational performance is governed by two orthogonal systems dimensions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Correlation Horizon ($\Delta t$) vs. False Positives:} During 50 simulated administrator maintenance windows ($\Delta t \in [10\text{s}, 45\text{s}]$), background \texttt{AutoEnroll} requests arrived concurrently inside the mutation window with probability $1 - e^{-\lambda \Delta t}$. At $\Delta t = 10\text{s}$, zero collisions occur ($0.0\%$ FPR, $100.0\%$ precision). Widening $\Delta t$ to $30\text{s}$ admits 3 Poisson collisions ($6.0\%$ FPR). For $\Delta t \ge 60\text{s}$, false positives plateau at exactly 4 / 50 ($8.00\%$ maintenance window FPR, $0.0395\%$ overall event FPR, $92.16\%$ precision at $\Delta_{\text{poll}}=5\text{s}$ and $92.31\%$ at $\Delta_{\text{poll}}=1\text{s}$) because all 50 administrative maintenance windows conclude within $45\text{s} < 60\text{s}$. Naive rollback monitors generated 50/50 false alarms ($100\%$ FPR, $19.35\%$ precision).
    \item \textbf{CA Database Polling Lag ($\Delta_{\text{poll}}$) vs. False Negatives:} Because KDC Event 4768 omits SubjectKeyIdentifier (SKID), linking Event 4887 to Event 4768 strictly requires CA Jet DB enrichment. When an attacker's PKINIT execution delay $\delta_{\text{pkinit}} \sim \text{LogNormal}(1.2, 0.8)$ executes before the next polling tick ($t_{\text{auth}} + \Delta_{\text{watermark}} < t_{\text{poll}}$), the join keys remain un-enriched at evaluation time, generating organic False Negatives. At $\Delta_{\text{poll}} = 1.0\text{s}$, CIPG achieves $100.00\%$ recall ($48/48$). At default $\Delta_{\text{poll}} = 5.0\text{s}$, CIPG achieves $97.92\%$ recall ($47/48$). At $\Delta_{\text{poll}} \in \{15\text{s}, 60\text{s}\}$, rapid attacks beat the polling cycle, resulting in $89.58\%$ recall ($43/48$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ4: Game-Theoretic Stackelberg Remediation Performance}
We evaluated our Co-Adaptive Stackelberg policy against three standard remediation baselines across 30 generated identity graphs:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Random Cut:} Selects arbitrary reachable edges within budget.
    \item \textbf{Degree Centrality:} Selects edges incident to the highest-degree nodes.
    \item \textbf{Static Greedy:} Ranks participation in initially selected shortest paths once, divided by assigned revocation cost, then cuts feasible edges in that order.
    \item \textbf{Co-Adaptive Stackelberg (Ours):} Applies the sequential reachability heuristic to the interdiction formulation under heterogeneous operational disruption costs and operational edge immutability ($E_b \subsetneq E$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Operational Immutability Plateau and the Runtime Defense Residual:}
In enterprise networks, a realistic fraction of directory edges represents immutable operational dependencies ($E_b \subsetneq E$). As shown in Table~\ref{tab:stackelberg_results} and Figure~\ref{fig:stackelberg_convergence}, proactive graph interdiction achieves \textbf{$98.5 \pm 4.0\%$} paths severed at $B_{\text{ops}}=24$ (vs. $97.6 \pm 6.5\%$ for Static Greedy).
This experiment leaves a mean residual of approximately $1.5\%$ under the selected graph instances, budgets, and non-blockable edges. It does not prove that every enterprise has an unavoidable residual of that size or that a different feasible policy cannot disconnect all modeled paths. Runtime monitoring is complementary to the evaluated hardening policies.

\paragraph{Scalability via Provenance-Cone Guidance:}
The historical depth-limited cone benchmark drops some queried source--target pairs. Its nominal $N=5,000$ graph actually contains 4,551 nodes, and its $1,246.3\times$ timing ratio compares different pair tasks. It therefore does not establish equal-task solver acceleration or online response latency.

We implement a path-preserving projection instead. For every requested pair $(s,t)$, retain the intersection of vertices reachable from $s$ and vertices that can reach $t$, together with every pair endpoint. Every original $s$--$t$ path is retained, so subsequent removal of any retained edge subset preserves that pair's reachability answer. Edge costs, relations and immutability are copied unchanged, and initially unconnected endpoints remain represented. Intersecting any full-graph cut with the retained edge set removes no requested-path effect and cannot increase its nonnegative cost, so the optimal budgeted pair-disconnection value is also preserved. A heuristic's selected cut can still depend on its search order. Shared endpoint closures are computed once per distinct endpoint.

The new benchmark retains all pairs, uses the same historical heuristic on full and projected graphs, and includes projection cost (Table~\ref{tab:scalability_bench}). At 5,000 actual nodes and 325 queried pairs, 874--890 nodes remain. Across fifteen alternating-order timing pairs, the median per-run ratio is $7.54\times$, with observed range $6.62$--$8.99\times$, and severed-pair outcomes agree in every run. The median full and projection-inclusive times are 3,448.15~ms and 439.29~ms; their ratio need not equal the median of individual ratios. This controlled result is narrower than the historical headline and does not supply an optimality or enterprise workload guarantee.
