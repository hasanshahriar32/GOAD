\begin{abstract}
\noindent Transient changes in Active Directory Certificate Services (ADCS) complicate investigation when issuance, authentication and configuration records expose different identifiers. We present CertGraph, combining static graph analysis, certificate-specific provenance and disruption-budgeted remediation. Its issuer-scoped evidence contract retains incomplete candidates, distinguishes possible from definite answers and revises links as evidence changes. A frozen 5,400-history confirmation retains 99.06 percent of true observed links as candidates and resolves 55.82 percent definitively on its randomized subset. Four intervention matrices and a prospective concurrent new-forest cohort provide 72 certificate cases: 66 successful exact-certificate authentications, including 13 administrative uses, and six certificate-bound rejections. Current-patch controls distinguish UPN-only rejection from SID-URL administrative use after template restoration. Actual Certipy and Sigma executions separate configuration risk from realized privilege escalation. A guarded SQLite implementation agrees with the identity reference at all 5,423 evaluated prefixes, establishing relational realizability rather than a unique reasoning primitive. A separate literal two-layer bilinear study reproduces 10,800 held-out predictions on family-disjoint inputs, with no classification advantage over matched additive attention and systematic long-path misses. A metadata-preserving remediation rerun checks 120 graph/budget instances; a path-preserving projection measures the same query task. Finally, a paired laboratory pilot quantifies collection cost and increased authentication process envelopes. The contribution is a reproducible certificate-evidence contract with controlled transfer, independent outcome checks and measured trade-offs.
\end{abstract}
