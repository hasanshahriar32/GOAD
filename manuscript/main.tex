\begin{abstract}
\noindent Active Directory Certificate Services (ADCS) forms the identity and public-key infrastructure backbone of enterprise Windows ecosystems. Despite its ubiquity, misconfigurations in certificate templates and enrollment access control lists (ACLs) yield catastrophic privilege escalation paths, enabling unprivileged domain principals to forge cryptographic certificates and authenticate as arbitrary administrative accounts. Current defensive methodologies suffer from two fundamental systemic vulnerabilities: (1) \textit{Static Snapshot Blindness}, where temporal mutation-and-rollback attacks (e.g., ESC4, ESC9) leave the directory in a bit-identical state ($\text{SHA-256}(G_{t_0}) = \text{SHA-256}(G_{t_3})$), rendering static graph scanners 100\% blind to active post-rollback certificate abuse; and (2) \textit{GNN Shortcut Memorization}, where standard graph neural networks memorize template attribute flags and collapse to $1.59\%$ accuracy ($1/63$) under adversarial counterfactual hard negatives. Furthermore, audit log inspection reveals an unaddressed systems-security vulnerability: the \textit{PKCS\#10 ASN.1 Subject Alternative Name (SAN) Concealment and CA--KDC Schema Discontinuity} ($\pi_{\text{CA}} \cap \pi_{\text{KDC}} = \emptyset$), wherein certificate issuance and Kerberos PKINIT authentications share zero common event identifiers.

To overcome these structural limitations, we introduce \textbf{CertGraph}, an integrated static-to-dynamic neuro-symbolic framework for ADCS escalation detection and autonomous interdiction. First, we formulate the \textbf{Multiplicative Bilinear Reachability Gate} ($\mathbf{g}_{uv} = \sigma(\mathbf{h}_u^\top \mathbf{W}_g \mathbf{h}_v)$), which strictly conditions neighborhood message-passing on topological path reachability and cures inductive shortcut collapse, achieving $92.06\%$ ($58/63$, $95\%$ Wilson CI: $[82.7\%, 96.6\%]$) classification accuracy on adversarial hard negatives (up from $1.59\%$ for additive skip baselines), with the remaining $7.94\%$ non-monotonic DACL \texttt{Explicit-Deny} overrides and deep-horizon edge cases resolved by Tier-2 symbolic verification. Second, we engineer the \textbf{Cryptographic Invariant Provenance Graph (CIPG)} powered by an asynchronous state-tracking automaton (Invariant~V) that bridges CA database records and KDC event streams across Subject Key Identifiers (SKID), certificate serials, and SHA-1 thumbprints. Under concurrent enterprise background traffic ($11,298$ events) with Poisson auto-enrollment collisions and CA Jet DB polling latency, CIPG achieves $95.83\%$ recall, $93.88\%$ precision ($94.85\%$ F1-score), and a $6.00\%$ false-positive rate across admin maintenance windows ($0.03\%$ overall event FPR), while delivering $69.04\%$ meta-node graph compression at $1,875.9$~events/sec. Finally, we formulate the \textbf{Disruption-Budgeted Stackelberg Remediation Game}, proving that Multi-Principal Access Interdiction (MPAI) is NP-complete via reduction from Directed Multiway Cut. Under realistic operational constraints where $8\%$ of enterprise permissions are immutable ($E_b \subsetneq E$), our co-adaptive Stackelberg policy dominates static greedy interdiction across budgets ($p < 0.001$), neutralizing $94.8 \pm 3.6\%$ of attack paths at $B_{\text{ops}}=24$ (vs. $89.1 \pm 5.2\%$ for greedy) while revoking $52.7\%$ fewer edges ($5.3$ vs. $11.2$ edges). The irreducible $5.2\%$ residual attack surface is defended at runtime by CIPG, and CIPG provenance-cone guidance delivers a $>180\times$ speedup ($<45$~ms vs. $>8,400$~ms at $N=5,000$ nodes).
\end{abstract}
