Md. Hasan Shahriar, Taufiq Biswas, and Md. Ramim Hossain
Abstract—Certificate-template restoration can hide the con-
figuration that enabled an earlier Active Directory Certificate
Services (ADCS) issuance while leaving the certificate usable.
CertGraph reconstructs issuance-to-authentication links through
an issuer-scoped evidence contract. It retains compatible can-
didates when identifiers are incomplete, distinguishes possible
from definite attribution, and revises conclusions when evidence
conflicts. A frozen study of 5,400 synthetic histories includes 2,000
randomized histories in which the contract retains 99.06% of
true observed links as candidates and resolves 55.82% definitively.
Four controlled Windows intervention matrices and a concurrent
forest workload provide 72 certificate cases: 66 successful exact-
certificate authentications, including 13 administrative uses, and
six certificate-bound rejections. Fixed signed requests isolate the
tested template-bit contrast, while current-patch controls distin-
guish UPN-only rejection from administrative use of SID-URL
certificates after restoration. A guarded SQLite implementation
agrees with the identity reference at all 5,423 evaluated prefixes.
Budgeted sequential interdiction supplies a complementary re-
mediation analysis on 120 graph/budget instances, with explicit
small-instance optimality gaps. An auxiliary graph-learning study
finds identical additive and bilinear classifications and misses
every tested long-path positive. The evidence supports certificate-
specific investigation in controlled laboratories and identifies
the collection, source-trust and deployment conditions requiring
further validation.
Index Terms—Active Directory security, certificate services,
provenance analysis, incomplete evidence, certificate authentica-
tion, network interdiction.
I. INTRODUCTION
Active Directory Certificate Services (ADCS) issues cer-
tificates that can be used for domain authentication through
PKINIT [1]. Different services record issuance and subsequent
use. When a certificate template is changed, used and restored
between scans, its current configuration no longer describes
the conditions under which an earlier certificate was issued.
Investigators must therefore connect directory changes, CA
records and KDC authentication outcomes.
SpecterOps documented certificate-service escalation tech-
niques [2]. Certificate mapping and the installed Windows
updates also affect whether a certificate is accepted for an
administrative identity [3]. We ask what certificate-specific
attribution is justified when identifiers or timing evidence are
incomplete, late or contradictory.

A. Certificate Issuance and the Evidence Gap
In an ADCS deployment, certificate issuance is governed
by Active Directory objects known as Certificate Templates
(stored in the LDAP configuration naming context). When
an enterprise template exposes an insecure combination
of attributes—such as the CT_FLAG_ENROLLEE_SUPPLIES_
SUBJECT bitflag alongside an Extended Key Usage (EKU)
permitting Client Authentication (1.3.6.1.5.5.7.3.2) or
Smart Card Logon (1.3.6.1.5.2.3.4), without requiring CA
administrator approval—any domain principal granted enroll-
ment permissions can request an X.509 certificate specifying
an arbitrary Subject Alternative Name (SAN). The attacker
can supply the user principal name (UPN) of a Domain
Administrator (e.g., Administrator@domain.local), obtain
a cryptographically valid certificate signed by the enterprise
root CA, and submit that certificate to the Kerberos Key
Distribution Center (KDC) via PKINIT to request a Ticket
Granting Ticket (TGT), when the KDC accepts the resulting
identity mapping. Certificate-binding policy and patch level
can prevent this outcome [3].
Configuration assessment and incident reconstruction an-
swer different questions. Certipy reports template and CA
risks [4]; a positive finding does not by itself show that a
certificate was used with elevated privileges. Whole-network
provenance systems connect activity across hosts [5, 6], but
a certificate-level explanation still needs an issuer-scoped link
between issuance and use.
This study examines three issues. First, a graph classifier can
learn template attributes that correlate with generated labels
while missing the access-control path. Such shortcut behavior
is a known learning problem [7]; our experiments test it under
a stated ADCS permission abstraction. Second, restored con-
figuration attributes cannot identify every intervening issuance.
Third, the captured CA and KDC records expose different cer-
tificate fields. Existing SAN-based CA/KDC queries provide
useful operational correlation [8, 9]. We examine what can still
be concluded when the required fields are absent or disagree.
B. Research Question and Contributions
The central question is whether an investigator can make a
certificate-specific issuance-to-use claim when configuration
has been restored and evidence is incomplete. CertGraph
treats identity linkage, temporal overlap and remediation as
separate decisions: a compatible identifier does not establish

II. RELATED WORK
Active Directory and PKI Security: The BloodHound
software models identity relationships for privilege-path in-
vestigation. SpecterOps [2] systematized ADCS misconfig-
urations; Certipy [4] supports certificate-service assessment.
A restored configuration snapshot alone cannot reveal every
intervening issuance. However, practical CA/KDC correlation
is established: In.security describes SAN-abuse investigation
in KQL [8], and Splunk publishes an ESC1 authentication
analytic [9]. CIPG therefore does not claim novelty for joining
issuance and authentication events. Its research question con-
cerns certificate-specific identity consistency, possible versus
definite answers, and revision under incomplete or conflicting
evidence.
Graph learning and shortcut behavior: Graph neural
networks aggregate information over relationships. The orig-
inal graph model and later reviews describe the assumptions
and model families behind this approach [10–12]. Convolu-
tional graph models are reviewed by Wu et al. [11]; the
attention construction follows GAT [13]. Relational graph
convolution (R-GCN) and gated graph neural networks are
alternative families reviewed in [11, 12]; GATv2 changes the
attention scoring order [14]. Our additive control is a matched
ablation, not a comparison against all these families. Euler
studies temporal link prediction for lateral movement [15]; its
task differs from our resolved-permission predicate. Shortcut
learning [7] and adversarial robustness [16] motivate testing
structural counterfactuals. Neither literature establishes that the
bilinear gate used here improves classification. Our matched
experiment finds no such improvement.
Provenance and incomplete evidence: Surveys describe
provenance capture, dependency growth and security anal-
ysis [17, 18]. HADES addresses whole-network AD inves-
tigation [5], while CPD examines persistence [6]. These
are related investigation systems, not executed baselines
in this study. Broader provenance-IDS comparisons include
HOLMES, KAIROS and FLASH; the recent evaluation-
oriented synthesis [19] distinguishes their alerting and analysis
units. These are literature comparisons, not reproduced base-
lines. Our identity-query experiment addresses a different task
from end-to-end host intrusion detection; it supplies no ranking
against those systems. Causality-preserving evaluation [20]
further motivates keeping certificate-link correctness separate
from attack reconstruction and detection. Established work on
order-incomplete queries [21], temporal constraints [22], and
record matching [23] supplies broader reasoning foundations.
Record matching alone does not guarantee correct attribution
under compromised logs. Our contribution is the certificate-
specific evidence contract and its evaluated boundaries, rather
than a new general entity-resolution or temporal-query primi-
tive. The timestamp-assumptions analysis [24] is a preprint; it
is not used as evidence of system performance.
Interdiction and remediation: Attack-graph analysis in-
cludes reachability, cost and uncertainty models [25]. Goel
et al. evaluate evolutionary policies for hardening AD
graphs [26]. Classical network interdiction studies flow re-
duction and shortest-path disruption [27, 28]. Security games
provide a broader decision framework [29], while known cut
problems provide the complexity foundation [30, 31]. These
objectives and sources are distinct. Our formulation adds
assigned disruption costs and immutable edges to the evaluated
instances. It does not establish a new general hardness result
or certify an optimal remediation policy. The exhaustive small-
instance comparison instead measures where the sequential
heuristic falls short.
III. ADCS THREAT MODEL AND SYSTEMATIC BLIND SPOTS
We consider an attacker with an ordinary domain foothold
and the template-modification or enrollment rights required
by the tested condition. The defender can collect directory,
CA and KDC records but may receive incomplete or delayed
fields. Correct raw issuance and authentication records are trust
assumptions; optional CA exports have a separately declared
fault budget. The experiments concern controlled issuance and
use, not the entire ADCS attack taxonomy.

IV. CRYPTOGRAPHIC INVARIANT PROVENANCE GRAPH (CIPG)
To overcome the static snapshot blind spot and the CA–
KDC schema discontinuity identified in Section III, we design
the Cryptographic Invariant Provenance Graph (CIPG)
(Figure 2). CIPG dynamically ingests raw Windows security
audit event streams and CA transactional records, maintaining
revisable lineage state across protocol layers. Provenance in-
gestion is independent of the optional graph classifier; neural-
negative cases are not discarded.
A. Multi-Source Provenance Ingestion and Entity Resolution
CIPG processes heterogeneous audit streams across three
disjoint operational layers:
1) Directory Service Audit Events (NTDS): Monitored
via Windows Security Event ID 5136 (Directory Ser-
vice Object Modified). Captures granular LDAP mu-
tations on certificate templates, user objects, and ac-
cess control lists, recording the modifying principal,
target object GUID, modified attribute (e.g., nTDSDSOp-
tions, pKIExtendedKeyUsage, msPKI-Certificate-
Name-Flag), and operation type (Value Added / Value
Deleted).
2) Certificate Authority Telemetry and CA DB Bridge:
Monitored via Event IDs 4886 (Certificate Request Re-
ceived) and 4887 (Certificate Issued). To bridge missing
shared certificate identifiers, CIPG queries the local CA
transactional database using certutil, requesting the
RequestID, SerialNumber, SubjectKeyIdentifier,
and CertificateHash fields, or the raw CA RPC in-
terface. This populates a persistent cryptographic tuple:
𝜏crypto = ⟨Issuer, RequestID, SKID,
SerialNumber, ThumbprintSHA1⟩

via Event ID 4768 (Kerberos Authentication Ticket Re-

the authenticating client name, domain target, certificate

a nonzero result is recorded separately as a rejection, not

Mathematical Guarantees and Operational Concurrency
Realities: A serial number is scoped to its issuer [32]; SKID
identifies a public key and can recur across distinct certificates.
A thumbprint identifies certificate bytes, while an unauthen-
ticated log field does not itself prove certificate possession
or source integrity. The reference implementation requires
all shared identity fields to agree and preserves compatible
incompletely identified issuances, so missing fields cannot
manufacture uniqueness. Late evidence can withdraw a pre-
viously resolved link. Retention enables delayed-use analysis
only while the necessary observations remain available; no
bounded-memory or irreversible-finality guarantee follows.
Bounded-time and source-fault reasoning: For opening,
issuance, and rollback intervals 𝑂, 𝐼, 𝑅, definite temporal
overlap requires 𝑂+ ≤𝐼−and 𝐼+ ≤𝑅−. Otherwise, a feasible
overlap remains possible. An exact difference-constraint ref-
erence additionally accepts explicitly justified operation-order
edges [21, 22]; contradictory constraints yield an inconsistent-
evidence answer. Cross-host record numbers do not supply
those edges. This is an application of established temporal
reasoning, with certificate-specific evidence requirements.
For a declared optional-export fault budget 𝑘, let H𝑘contain
every deletion of at most 𝑘CA bridge records. Candidate
union over H𝑘preserves alternative explanations; a certain link
must be resolved to the same issuance in every hypothesis.
Abstaining hypotheses are retained, rather than removed to
force agreement. If the true issuance is captured, raw is-
suance/KDC fields and time bounds are correct, and at most 𝑘
bridge records are faulty, deleting those faulty records leaves
a hypothesis containing the true candidate. This is a condi-
tional retention property, not a claim of certain resolution or
robustness to compromised raw audit sources. The exhaustive
implementation is a correctness reference with combinatorial
cost, and source-level compromise can invalidate a record-level
budget [23, 24].
The synthetic replay evaluates configured maintenance win-
dows, background renewals and polling delays. Its collision
counts describe that generated workload, not a universal arrival
law. Section VI-B gives the actual sampling scheme and
separates matching-template collisions from the total renewal
count.
E. Graph Reduction and Meta-Node Compacting

C. Adaptive and Static Greedy Heuristics
We compare simple degree-based and cost-aware static
shortest-path-participation heuristics. The latter computes ini-
tial path participation divided by assigned edge cost once,
so it already accounts for cost. Its limitation is the fixed
ranking: earlier cuts can make later candidates redundant, and
new shortest paths can change which permissions matter. The
adaptive policy recomputes this information after each chosen
cut. Degree-only ranking is a distinct baseline and need not
have the same cost sensitivity. These are implemented research
comparators rather than measured deployments of an industrial
remediation product.
The evaluated adaptive sequential policy recomputes active
shortest paths and candidate marginal pair-disconnection gains
after each cut. It can select low-cost enrollment edges when
they lie on relevant paths. Removing a candidate temporarily
must preserve every edge attribute when the candidate is
restored; relation and immutability metadata affect subsequent
scoring. The implementation supplies no global optimum or
approximation guarantee. Exact small-instance comparisons
quantify its observed suboptimality.

C. RQ3: Budgeted Sequential Interdiction
We evaluated the adaptive sequential policy against three
standard remediation baselines across 30 generated identity
graphs:
1) Random Cut: Selects arbitrary reachable edges within
budget.
2) Degree Centrality: Selects edges incident to the highest-
degree nodes.
3) Static Greedy: Ranks participation in initially selected
shortest paths once, divided by assigned revocation cost,
then cuts feasible edges in that order.
4) Adaptive sequential: Applies the sequential reachability
heuristic to the interdiction formulation under heteroge-
neous operational disruption costs and operational edge
immutability (𝐸𝑏⊊𝐸).
Operational Immutability Plateau and the Runtime De-
fense Residual: In enterprise networks, a realistic fraction of
directory edges represents immutable operational dependen-
cies (𝐸𝑏⊊𝐸). As shown in Table XII and Figure 3, proactive
graph interdiction achieves 98.5±4.0% paths severed at 𝐵ops =
24 (vs. 97.6±6.5% for Static Greedy). This experiment leaves a
mean residual of approximately 1.5% under the selected graph
instances, budgets, and non-blockable edges. It does not prove
that every enterprise has an unavoidable residual of that size or
that a different feasible policy cannot disconnect all modeled
paths. Runtime monitoring is complementary to the evaluated
hardening policies.
Paired tests and multiple comparisons:
Independent
adjacency-list checks validate all 120 budget/cut outcomes
and 11,100 reachability queries. Table XII compares adaptive
and static greedy on the same 30 graphs using a paired 𝑡-
test and exact signed-rank enumeration. The eight-test Holm
family covers four budgets and two procedures [38]. Budget 6
survives both procedures; budget 18 survives only exact signed
rank (adjusted 𝑝= 0.02344, paired-𝑡adjusted 𝑝= 0.05138).
At budget 24, only five paired differences are nonzero and all
are positive. Exact enumeration over 25 = 32 sign assignments
gives raw 𝑝= 0.0625; the mean gain is 0.94 percentage
points. Both adjusted budget-24 values are 𝑝= 0.25. The
measurements therefore do not support a significant budget-
24 difference. Ranks use exact rational disconnection fractions
and omit zero differences. This is a descriptive synthetic-graph
analysis, not evidence of enterprise operational superiority.
Revoked-edge count: Under 𝐵ops = 24, Static Greedy
revokes an average of 10.9 edges (10.9 ± 0.9), under the as-
signed heterogeneous costs. The saved summary does not mea-
sure the resulting business-workflow disruption. The metadata-
preserving adaptive rerun revokes an average of 5.7 edges
(5.7 ± 2.3), a 47.7% reduction in revoked-edge count. The
disruption weights are assigned model parameters; actual ad-
ministrator effort and business disruption were not measured.
Scalability via Provenance-Cone Guidance: We imple-
ment a path-preserving projection. For every requested pair
(𝑠, 𝑡), retain the intersection of vertices reachable from 𝑠and
vertices that can reach 𝑡, together with every pair endpoint.
Every original 𝑠–𝑡path is retained, so subsequent removal
of any retained edge subset preserves that pair’s reachability
answer. Edge costs, relations and immutability are copied
unchanged, and initially unconnected endpoints remain repre-
sented. Intersecting any full-graph cut with the retained edge
set removes no requested-path effect and cannot increase its
nonnegative cost, so the optimal budgeted pair-disconnection
value is also preserved. A heuristic’s selected cut can still
depend on its search order. Shared endpoint closures are
computed once per distinct endpoint.
The equal-query benchmark retains all pairs, uses the
same sequential heuristic on full and projected graphs, and
includes projection cost (Table XIII). At 5,000 actual nodes
and 325 queried pairs, 874–890 nodes remain. Across fifteen
alternating-order timing pairs, the median per-run ratio is
7.54×, with observed range 6.62–8.99×, and severed-pair
outcomes agree in every run. The median full and projection-
inclusive times are 3,448.15 ms and 439.29 ms; their ratio
need not equal the median of individual ratios. This controlled
result does not supply an optimality or enterprise workload
guarantee.

C. Threats to Validity and Limitations
We distinguish the following threats to validity and experi-
mental limitations:
1) Evidence Scale and Label Validity: The archive’s
scenario names do not establish seven successful attacks.
Four final fixed-request matrices supply 42 successful
exact-certificate authentications and six certificate-bound
rejections, with nine privileged uses and matched self-
use controls. A prospective transfer cohort adds 24
distinct certificates, including four further privileged
uses: the combined primary studies contain 72 certificate
cases, 66 successful uses and six rejections. The transfer
cohort has new identity namespaces and concurrent
multi-template activity but shares the Windows image
family. They establish controlled outcomes, not enter-
prise prevalence. Independent Windows 2019 builds
17763.4974 and 17763.9376 are measured. Current-

additional post-rollback workflow prevents attributing
every old/new difference solely to the update. Eigh-

supplemented by a later read-only snapshot; original

VIII. CONCLUSION
CertGraph links certificate issuance and use under an ex-
plicit issuer-scoped evidence contract. Its live evaluation veri-
fies 66 successful certificate authentications and six certificate-
bound rejections, including 13 administrative uses. The
current-patch controls show why a restored template scan and
a certificate-use investigation answer different questions. The
identity contract can also be implemented using guarded SQL;
the evaluated contribution concerns its security requirements
and evidence handling rather than a unique reasoning primi-
tive.
The graph-learning results do not establish a classification
advantage for bilinear gating. Both matched literal models
miss the tested long-path positives, so a verifier that exam-
ines only neural-positive cases remains incomplete for this
workload. The remediation experiments also show heuristic
optimality gaps and budget-dependent differences from greedy
selection. Larger deployments require separate measurements
of collection cost, retained history and administrator-assigned
disruption costs. The present results support these specific
conclusions within the recorded laboratory and generator con-
ditions.

APPENDIX B
AUXILIARY GRAPH-LEARNING EVALUATION
A. In-Distribution Ablations and Perturbation Asymmetry
We first distinguish the class-balanced generator from the
attribution and live-policy experiments. Their labels, model
implementations, and observation units differ.
Skip-connection ablation: The saved five-fold artifact re-
ports Macro-F1 0.9986±0.0029 with skips and 0.4768±0.0239
without skips. Recomputing the paired statistic from the five
saved fold values gives 𝑡= 46.245, 𝑝= 1.308 × 10−6,
and four degrees of freedom. The dispersions are population
standard deviations across these folds. Shared training data and
generator assumptions limit interpretation of this descriptive
comparison. This ablation is not an empirical validation of
the conditional derivative analysis in Appendix A.
Class Proportions and Probability Interpretation: The
static experiments reported here are generator-based classifi-
cation studies. Their balanced labels do not establish a 96%
enterprise safe base rate, and their softmax scores are not
calibrated probabilities of real-world compromise. We retain
probabilities and selected checkpoints in the matched audit so
that classification errors can be checked independently.
Perturbation Asymmetry and Simple Comparators: The
saved robustness artifact gives Macro-F1 0.9479 after 30%
edge perturbation, compared with 0.8965 and 0.7709 after
5% and 10% feature perturbation. This sensitivity pattern
motivates testing structural counterfactuals; it does not prove
that every prediction ignores topology. A separately verified,
previously saved three-seed ESC13 abstraction also favors
simple graph-count models over the existing GNN on its IID
cases. Its symbolic reference defines the labels, so its perfect
agreement is a consistency result rather than independent
exploit validation.
B. Generalization and Hard-Negative Diagnostics
A source audit identifies two problems with interpreting the
original-label 41/63 safe-label agreement as a general hard-
negative detection result. Ten “deep horizon” cases retain a
membership path to an allowed enrollment group and therefore
are positive under a transitive effective-permission interpreta-
tion. The stored 22 disagreements are eight administrator-only
write cases, four deny-write cases, seven benign OID links,
and three deep OID links; the disagreements are reported
using their stored categories. The canonical generator also
repeats inputs across training and validation. These original-
label counts are retained in the artifact but do not establish
independent-case specificity or a binomial confidence interval.
We therefore freeze and execute a matched 2 × 2 diag-
nostic: additive versus feature-channel-gated implementations,
each with counterfactual augmentation probability zero or 0.3,
across three independently selected seeds. Model equations,
propagation depth, and original artifacts remain unchanged.

APPENDIX C
ARTIFACT CORRECTIONS AND EXCLUDED RUNS
The retained artifact distinguishes source captures, adjudica-
tion views and derived results. Exclusions are methodological,
not additional negative policy outcomes:
• A first split-actor capture failed and is excluded; the
outcome-checked pilot has a 14-second post-rollback de-
lay, not a 24-hour delay.
• A development matrix contains two RPC failures among
twelve attempts. A fixed-request collector stops on a
pending-request-store key-binding failure. These attempts
are separate from the four final matrices and their 48
outcome-checked cases.
• An independent-forest setup with a four-hour clock differ-
ence is excluded. The accepted 2019 inventory is build
17763.4974 before update and 17763.9376 afterward; the
restored GOAD nodes are Windows Server 2016 build
14393.
• An empty System-log query suppresses eighteen contem-
poraneous CA exports. A later read-only CA snapshot
supplies these exports only in separate adjudication views.
It does not reconstruct missing historical template state
or correct the failed audit-clock model.
• A collection-pilot attempt exports final outcome evidence
too early. The accepted timing study waits for outcome
verification after the measured interval and validates all
48 authentications.
• In the interdiction scoring control, temporary edge dele-
tion restores only cost and loses other attributes. Restor-
ing every attribute changes nine of 120 disconnection
outcomes and 24 cut counts. The primary table uses
full restoration. In the separate 60-case exact audit, the
uncorrected control matches the optimum in 46 cases,
versus 43 for full restoration; a correctness fix is not
claimed as an accuracy improvement.
• A depth-limited cone timing compares different queried
pairs and uses 4,551 actual nodes for a nominal 5,000-
node graph. Its 1, 246.3× ratio is excluded from the
speedup claim. The reported projection benchmark retains
identical queried pairs and includes construction cost.
The hard-negative diagnostic contains 41/63 safe-label agree-
ments under its original labels; it is not a zero-agreement result.
Ten deep-horizon cases are positive under transitive enrollment.
Appendix B reports corrected labels, repeated-input limitations
and separate family-disjoint evaluation rather than using this
count as independent specificity.
