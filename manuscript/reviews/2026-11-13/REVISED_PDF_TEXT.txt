CertGraph: Cryptographic Invariant Provenance and
Multiplicative Bilinear Gating for Active Directory
Certificate Services Escalation Interdiction
Md. Hasan Shahriar, Taufiq Biswas, and Md. Ramim Hossain

Abstract—An ADCS certificate can remain usable after the
template that enabled its issuance has been restored. Investigating
that sequence requires evidence from directory changes, certificate issuance and domain authentication, whose records do not
always expose matching identifiers. CertGraph combines graph
analysis, certificate-specific provenance and remediation under an
assigned disruption budget. Its evidence contract scopes certificate identity to the issuer, retains compatible candidates when
fields are missing, and revises attribution when later records
conflict. We evaluate the contract using synthetic histories and
controlled Windows experiments. In a frozen 5,400-history study,
the randomized subset retains 99.06% of true observed links as
candidates and resolves 55.82% definitively. Four intervention
matrices and a separate concurrent forest workload provide 72
certificate cases: 66 successful exact-certificate authentications,
including 13 administrative uses, and six certificate-bound rejections. Current-patch controls distinguish rejection of UPNonly requests from administrative use of SID-URL certificates
after template restoration. A guarded SQLite implementation
agrees with the identity reference at all 5,423 evaluated prefixes.
Separate graph-learning experiments reproduce 10,800 held-out
predictions but find no classification advantage for bilinear gating
over matched additive attention. The remediation evaluation
checks 120 graph/budget instances and exposes heuristic optimality gaps. These results support certificate-specific investigation
under explicit evidence assumptions; they do not establish general
deployment effectiveness.

A. Certificate Issuance and the Evidence Gap

In an ADCS deployment, certificate issuance is governed
by Active Directory objects known as Certificate Templates
(stored in the LDAP configuration naming context). When
an enterprise template exposes an insecure combination
of attributes—such as the CT_FLAG_ENROLLEE_SUPPLIES_
SUBJECT bitflag alongside an Extended Key Usage (EKU)
permitting Client Authentication (1.3.6.1.5.5.7.3.2) or
Smart Card Logon (1.3.6.1.5.2.3.4), without requiring CA
administrator approval—any domain principal granted enrollment permissions can request an X.509 certificate specifying
an arbitrary Subject Alternative Name (SAN). The attacker
can supply the user principal name (UPN) of a Domain
Administrator (e.g., Administrator@domain.local), obtain
a cryptographically valid certificate signed by the enterprise
root CA, and submit that certificate to the Kerberos Key
Distribution Center (KDC) via PKINIT to request a Ticket
Granting Ticket (TGT), when the KDC accepts the resulting
identity mapping. Certificate-binding policy and patch level
can prevent this outcome [3].

output. The attenuation statement therefore does not prove
universal elimination of shortcuts. The historical numerical
implementation uses a feature-channel gate rather than this
literal scorer. We now implement a separate two-layer, fourhead realization of the displayed bilinear edge and self-skip
equations and evaluate it with identical-initialization additive
controls and matched augmentation (Table II). All relevant
parameter families are trainable through autograd. The two
implementations have identical held-out classifications in that
new study, and both miss the positive long-path cases. This
validates an auditable realization of the formulation, rather
than a universal shortcut-elimination or authorization guarantee. A second-tier verifier must process unresolved and out-ofhorizon cases, including neural negatives, if it is to recover
those observed misses.

C. Co-Adaptive Stackelberg vs. Static Greedy Interdiction
We compare simple degree-based and cost-aware static
shortest-path-participation heuristics. The latter computes initial path participation divided by assigned edge cost once,
so it already accounts for cost. Its limitation is the fixed
ranking: earlier cuts can make later candidates redundant, and
new shortest paths can change which permissions matter. The
adaptive policy recomputes this information after each chosen
cut. Degree-only ranking is a distinct baseline and need not
have the same cost sensitivity. These are implemented research
comparators rather than measured deployments of an industrial
remediation product.
The evaluated Co-Adaptive Stackelberg policy is a sequential heuristic for the stated formulation. It recomputes active
shortest paths and candidate marginal pair-disconnection gains,
prioritizing assigned low-cost structural chokepoints (Enroll
and EnablesEnrollment edges on the CA boundary, 𝑐 = 1.0)
that can sever multiple downstream escalation chains. This
implementation is not a branch-and-cut solver and supplies
neither a global optimum nor an equilibrium certificate. The
reachability utility shown above does not depend on which
surviving shortest path the follower selects; the experiments
evaluate that reachability objective rather than a separately
validated strategic equilibrium.

B. Discussion and Operational Considerations
a) Deployment in 24/7 Enterprise SOC Environments::
The legacy synthetic replay reports 13,192.4 events/sec and
6.91 MB on a fixed stream. Those measurements do not establish zero interference with domain authentication, bounded
history storage, or end-to-end CA polling cost. The lineage
reference recomputes retained evidence, and the fault-budget
reference enumerates deletion hypotheses. The recorded live
command envelopes and offline inference timings quantify two
parts of this cost: median recorded trial envelopes are 50.91 s
and 32.14 s in the two environments, while median offline
identity reasoning is 10.99 ms and 9.18 ms. A three-pair
collection pilot measures periodic export/reference updates
and verifies 48 legitimate authentications. The paired median
client process-envelope increases are 48.6–100.3 ms, with
sampled Python collector RSS of 56.5–59.5 MiB, excluding
children and the VM. This quantifies a short isolated-lab
workload; continuous high-load operation, lifetime retention
and production interference remain unestablished.
b) Cloud-Hybrid Identity Extensions:: The experiments
cover on-premises ADCS and KDC records. Extending the evidence contract to Microsoft Entra ID would require additional
identity-mapping and audit-source assumptions. Cross-tenant
tokens and synchronization-account compromise are outside
the evaluated workload. They should be treated as separate
validation tasks before making claims about hybrid identity
protection.
C. Threats to Validity and Limitations
We distinguish the following threats to validity and experimental limitations:
1) Evidence Scale and Label Validity: The archive’s
scenario names do not establish seven successful attacks.
Four final fixed-request matrices supply 42 successful
exact-certificate authentications and six certificate-bound
rejections, with nine privileged uses and matched selfuse controls. A prospective new-forest cohort adds 24
distinct certificates, including four further privileged
uses: the combined primary studies contain 72 certificate
cases, 66 successful uses and six rejections. The transfer
cohort has new identity namespaces and concurrent
multi-template activity but shares the Windows image
family. They establish controlled outcomes, not enterprise prevalence. Independent Windows 2019 builds
17763.4974 and 17763.9376 are measured. Currentpatch controls reveal request-form differences, but the
additional post-rollback workflow prevents attributing
every old/new difference solely to the update. Eighteen missing contemporaneous CA exports are explicitly
supplemented by a later read-only snapshot; original
captures remain intact. Original source images remain
read-only.
2) Static Model and Generator Validity: The matched
audit corrects reachable-path labels and checks 6,000
checkpoint predictions. Repeated training/validation
archetypes, fixed first-step transforms, hand-initialized
projections, and the distinction between feature-channel

20

and bilinear gating restrict what its numbers establish. A
separate literal bilinear study removes those implementation and repeated-whole-input problems with trainable
encoders and typed family-disjoint splits. Both literal
bilinear and additive attention still miss all 450 longpath positives per arm, without a classification advantage
for gating. A verifier restricted to neural positives cannot
repair these misses, and symbolic label agreement is not
independent Windows ground truth.
3) Parametric Disruption Costs vs. Interactive Administrator Feedback: In our Stackelberg remediation game,
operational disruption costs 𝑐(𝑒) are assigned parametrically according to relation type (𝑐(MemberOf) ∈
[2.8, 3.8], 𝑐(GenericAll) ∈ [2.0, 3.0], 𝑐(Enroll) ∈
[1.0, 1.4]) and operational edge immutability constraints
(𝐸 𝑏 ⊊ 𝐸). In production enterprise deployments, operational criticality is context-sensitive and evolving.
The exact small-instance audit gives concrete optimum
comparisons rather than a universal heuristic guarantee.
A metadata-preserving rerun also checks all 120 original
graph/budget instances; it changes nine objective outcomes and 24 cut counts. The primary table and revokededge comparison now use that corrected implementation.
Integrating real-time human-in-the-loop preference elicitation [19] requires a separate evaluation.
4) Trust, Time, and Necessary Causation: Candidate
retention under a bridge fault budget assumes correct
raw issuance/KDC records and captured true issuance.
Consistently forged raw histories defeat the methods,
while faults exceeding the declared budget invalidate
its retention guarantee. A counterexample shows that
self-consistent false order can also produce a wrong
definite overlap. A grouped-source reference handles the
six-record correlated-export control under an explicit
one-source assumption, which differs from a record
budget. The operation-order pilot uses the controlled
orchestrator as an additional trusted source and does not
repair the failed Windows audit-clock model. Temporal
overlap alone does not establish necessity. The new
matched CSR/key interventions support a conditional
contrast under the recorded policy fields and controlled
CA refresh, while unobserved concurrent changes and
missing restoration audit events remain distinct evidence
concerns.
D. Conclusion
CertGraph links certificate issuance and use under an explicit issuer-scoped evidence contract. Its live evaluation verifies 66 successful certificate authentications and six certificatebound rejections, including 13 administrative uses. The
current-patch controls show why a restored template scan and
a certificate-use investigation answer different questions. The
identity contract can also be implemented with guarded SQL;
the evaluated contribution lies in its security requirements and
evidence handling rather than a unique reasoning primitive.
The graph-learning results do not establish a classification
advantage for bilinear gating. Both matched literal models

CERTGRAPH: MANUSCRIPT FOR REVIEW

miss the tested long-path positives, so a verifier that examines
only neural-positive cases remains incomplete for this workload. The remediation experiments likewise show heuristic
optimality gaps and budget-dependent differences from greedy
selection. Larger deployments require separate measurements
of collection cost, retained history and administrator-assigned
disruption costs. The present results support these specific
conclusions within the recorded laboratory and generator conditions.
