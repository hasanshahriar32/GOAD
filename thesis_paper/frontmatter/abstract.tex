\vspace{0.3cm}
\noindent Active Directory Certificate Services (ADCS) forms the cryptographic backbone of enterprise identity, issuing credentials for domain authentication and single sign-on. Misconfigured certificate templates, access control lists (ACLs), and issuance policies introduce systemic privilege escalation vectors (ESC1--ESC15+) that enable unprivileged accounts to compromise entire Active Directory domains. Signature-based scanners (e.g., Certipy, BloodHound) evaluate flags in isolation and incur high traversal overhead on large topologies. In this thesis, we present \textbf{CertGraph}, a heterogeneous Graph Attention Network (Hetero-GAT) that models enterprise identity as a typed multigraph $G = (V, E, \mathcal{T}_V, \mathcal{T}_E)$ to detect structural escalation paths across core vectors (ESC1, ESC2, ESC3, ESC4, ESC9, and ESC13).

Grounding our evaluation in the security machine learning framework of Arp et al. (USENIX Security 2022), we identify and resolve three systemic pitfalls in synthetic identity datasets: positional index leakage, baseline feature asymmetry, and rule saturation. We prove mathematically (Theorem~1) that residual skip-connections are indispensable for preventing representation collapse on identity graphs; omitting them makes template representations independent of input flags, reducing classification Macro-F1 from 0.9986 to 0.4768. On an audited 700-environment enterprise benchmark evaluated under 5-fold cross-validation with Nadeau-Bengio corrected variance, CertGraph achieves a Macro-F1 of 0.9986, substantially outperforming flat classifiers (0.8600) and signature heuristics (0.7791).

However, when evaluated against an adversarial Zero-Shot Hard Negative suite ($n = 63$) containing benign templates with dangerous flags, standard additive skip GNNs collapse to 0.00\% accuracy ($0/63$) due to inductive shortcut learning. To cure this failure mode, we introduce Multiplicative Bilinear Reachability Gating, elevating hard-negative accuracy to \textbf{65.08\%} ($41/63$, $95\%$ Wilson CI: $[52.8\%, 75.7\%]$), while the remaining $34.92\%$ multi-attribute semantic crosstalk edge cases are resolved by Tier-2 symbolic verification to achieve $100\%$ precision. Furthermore, to resolve the fundamental blind spot of static snapshots against transient state mutations (where we empirically prove bit-identical state $\text{SHA-256}(G_{t_0}) \equiv \text{SHA-256}(G_{t_3})$ across all 39 templates and 0\% static recall), we elevate the symbolic verifier into a Cross-Layer Identity and PKI Provenance Graph (CIPG). We discover the ASN.1 SAN Concealment protocol vulnerability and CA--KDC schema discontinuity ($\pi_{\text{CA}} \cap \pi_{\text{KDC}} = \emptyset$), resolve it via a continuous CA Database Bridge detecting 7/7 live lab attacks, and achieve \textbf{97.92\%--100.00\% recall, 92.16\%--92.31\% precision (94.95\%--96.00\% F1-score)}, and an $8.00\%$ false-positive rate across 50 admin maintenance windows ($0.0395\%$ overall event FPR) under a $10,182$-event enterprise concurrent stream, while demonstrating $76.61\%$ meta-node graph compression at $13,192.4$ events/sec. Finally, we model identity privilege revocation as a Stackelberg security game, prove its NP-hardness via reduction from Directed Multiway Cut, and demonstrate a co-adaptive interdiction policy that strictly dominates greedy baselines under operational budget constraints ($p = 0.0431$). Under realistic operational edge immutability ($E_b \subsetneq E$), proactive interdiction neutralizes \textbf{98.5\% $\pm$ 4.0\%} of attack paths at $B_{\text{ops}}=24$ (vs. $97.6\% \pm 6.5\%$ for greedy) with $45.0\%$ fewer revocations ($6.0$ vs. $10.9$ edges), leaving an irreducible $\sim 1.5\%$ residual attack surface that must be defended at runtime by CIPG, while CIPG provenance-cone guidance delivers a \textbf{$1,246.3\times$ speedup} ($2.6$~ms vs. $3,181.5$~ms at $N=5,000$ nodes with 325 active attacker paths).
